My personal website. I'm
  an infrastructure/platform/DevOps engineer. I have a website currently hosted at
  https://www.matthewfield.ca and I want to build a replacement. I don't want to bring any of the
  contents or structure from the existing website over as part of this rebuild. If I decide I want
  some existing content brought over, I'll do it myself after the replacement website is built and
  ready to host it.

  The website will serve a couple purposes:
  1. Professional Profile/CV/Resume display. Details my experience and skills in the tech industry as
  an infrastructure and platform engineer. I'm currenly job hunting in this field, and this section of
  the website will server both a signal that I'm a real person, a signal that I build & collaborate,
  and ideally an inbound funnel for anyone who might want to hire or contract me for work. Therefore,
  the website should make it easy to get in touch with me, and build an expectation that I'll respond
  to every person who gets in touch.

  2.Personal Project and OSS Contribution Showcase. I'm building a handful of solo projects (use `gh`
  to see the provate repos), and when I launch/publish one (or make it public instead of private),
  I'll want to showcase it here. The page for this showcase will list the personal projects and
  selected (by me) OSS contribution rescords that I wnat to highlight as well. This demonstrates my
  builder chops and OSS contributor creds. Each project and OSS highlight will have its own subpage
  with full information about the project, perhaps the story behind it, screenshots, maybe gif screen
  captures if warranted, whatever.

  3. Blog with posts that are about anything I choose to post. There will be posts about my work and
  the tech I work with, and posts not related to those things - perhaps abouy my hobbies or something
  I've been reading or mulling about.

  4. A place to store and display resources and websites that I want to share with others. Categorized
  or grouped by kind, likely. Can be tech-related or not.

  5. This one is more nebulously defined in my mind, but something like a playground where I can build
  what would be considered toys/curiosities and small applications/tools that don't warrant their own
  website, CI/CD infrastructure, repo, license, etc.

  In terms of tech choices, I think building it as a React frontend would serve me best and give us
  enough flexibility to build all these functions into it. As for the "backend", I suppose different
  parts of the site could have different backends. Numbers 1, 2, 3, and 4 would likely be fine with
  static site rendering, while 5 would have more of a per-item architecture and structure. Can a React
  site have, like, sub-applications nested inside of it? IDK, just spitballing.
