I've been building an open-source stock & options trading journal app for months; something lightweight but not thin, something a community could build together, something well-designed and easy to use. And, most importantly, something free.

The plan for Tradr's hosted offering was to open it when it felt like a v1.0.

It never felt like v1.0.\
It still doesn't.

But,

::lead[It launches to the public Thursday, August 27.]

_(sign up for the [Tradr mailing list](https://www.tradr.cloud/newsletter) and you'll get a reminder.)_

Is it perfect? **No.**\
Does it have every feature I wish it could? **Not even close.**\
Is it pretty? Well, yes, actually I think it's quite nice on the eyes.

Point is: we've all heard "don't let perfect be the enemy of good", "ship early and get feedback", and so on. The solopreneurs I follow are constantly harping not to let planning prevent you from actually _doing_.

So I've got three days to tighten up a few last things and fluff the pillows before sign-ups open at [tradr.cloud](https://app.tradr.cloud/register).

If you simply cannot wait and know your way around a `docker` CLI, you can host it yourself because Tradr is [open-source](https://github.com/madmatt112/tradr) and built with self-hosted defaults. [Read the how-to here.](https://docs.tradr.cloud/self-hosting/docker-compose/)
