### WARNING: Tag-vs-category dominance test does not falsify a 2:1 weight
- **File:** /home/mcf/repo/matthew-field.ca/src/lib/blog.test.ts:327
- **Issue:** Success criterion states 'removing the 3:1 weight (changing to 2:1) fails the tag-vs-category dominance case'. The test at lines 327-365 constructs tagHeavy=(1 shared tag, 0 cats) and catHeavy=(0 tags, 3 shared cats) and asserts order [catHeavy, tagHeavy]. Under weight=3:1, scores tie at 3 and date tiebreak (catHeavy 2026-01-02 > tagHeavy 2026-01-01) picks catHeavy first. Under weight=2:1, tagHeavy=2 and catHeavy=3 → catHeavy still first on score. Order is identical in both regimes, so flipping the weight constant does not flip the assertion. The author's own comment (lines 361-365) acknowledges this. To actually falsify a 2:1 weight, use a (1 tag, 2 cats) vs (0 tags, 3 cats) candidate pair: under 3:1 the tag-heavy scores 3+2=5 vs 3 → tag-heavy wins; under 2:1 it scores 2+2=4 vs 3 → tag-heavy still wins. The clean falsifier is (1 tag, 0 cats) vs (0 tags, 2 cats): 3:1 → 3 vs 2 (tag wins); 2:1 → 2 vs 2 (cat wins on date/slug tiebreak depending on setup). The current test passes but doesn't enforce the weight magnitude as the spec demands.
- **Task Requirement:** Success criterion: removing the 3:1 weight (changing to 2:1) fails the tag-vs-category dominance case
